The rules are different here.

The app can help a community without owning it.

$

No Ads

  • Your attention is not for sale
  • No feed tuned to keep you clicking

Censorship Resistant

  • No company can ban you from Hydra
  • You decide whom to hear

Ownerless Communities

  • No owner, app, or server controls a topic
  • /h/science lives wherever people use it

Chosen Moderation

  • Borrow blocks, silences, and pins from people you trust
  • Ignore them or make your own

Local First

  • Drafts, lists, and private notes stay on your computer
  • Only what you publish leaves it
{ }

Open Source

  • Change Hydra or build a compatible app
  • Compatibility matters; permission does not

Familiar Topics

A Hydra topic can shadow a Reddit community. If you know r/science, you already know where to find /h/science. That remains true if Reddit no longer lets you take part there.

The bridge is optional. Hydra does not need Reddit to work.

Calmer Feeds

There is no mystery ranking system trying to hold your attention. Choose how to sort the feed, then get on with it.

  • No global karmaA popularity score does not follow you from topic to topic.
  • Clear feed choicesSort by New, Top, Discussed, Controversial, or Revisited.
  • Private RevisitSet something aside without announcing it to anyone.
  • Separate personasKeep unrelated parts of your online life separate.

Your community can leave.

Hydra is free software on an open protocol. If this app goes wrong, people can fix it, fork it, or use another compatible one.
